 # Short Intro
  -   Magnets are distinguished materials that produce a magnetic field through the precise alignment and motion of electrons within their atomic structure. Each possesses a north and south pole, with opposite poles attracting and like poles repelling. Magnetic behavior may be classified as ferromagnetic, exemplified by iron or neodymium; paramagnetic, exhibiting subtle attraction; or diamagnetic, experiencing gentle repulsion. Their applications are both profound and pervasive, encompassing electric motors, generators, magnetic storage, and magnetic resonance imaging. At the atomic level, magnetism derives from electron spin and orbital motion, and when atomic magnetic moments achieve concerted alignment, the material exhibits a coherent and manifest macroscopic magnetization.

## Types of magnets

### **Permanent magnets**
  -   Neodymium (NdFeB) – very strong, used in speakers, motors, etc.
  -   Samarium-Cobalt (SmCo) – strong and heat-resistant.
  -   Alnico – made of aluminum, nickel, and cobalt; used in instruments.
  -   Ceramic (Ferrite) – cheap and widely used (fridge magnets, motors).
    <!-- there are soft and hard ferrite --> 

  ### **Temporary magnets**
   - Soft iron (used in electromagnets, relays, transformers).

  ### Electromagnets
  Magnets created by electricity — a coil of wire with current passing through it.
- Ferromagnetic (strongly magnetic, like iron, cobalt, nickel)
- Paramagnetic (weakly attracted, like aluminum)
- Diamagnetic (weakly repelled, like copper, bismuth)

## Magnet physics in short
- Magnets work because of the movement of electric charges, specifically electrons in atoms. In magnetic materials, the spin of electrons creates tiny magnetic fields, and when these fields align, they create a larger, unified magnetic field. This alignment results in the creation of a magnetic force, which can attract or repel other magnetic objects.
